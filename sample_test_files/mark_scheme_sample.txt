GCSE PE Paper 1 - Energy Systems Assessment
Mark Scheme

Total Marks: 20

Question 1: Describe the aerobic energy system (3 marks)
Assessment Objective: AO1 - Knowledge
Topic: Energy Systems
Keywords: oxygen, aerobic, ATP, mitochondria, glucose
Acceptable Answer:
- The aerobic system uses oxygen to break down glucose
- It produces ATP (adenosine triphosphate) for energy
- It occurs in the mitochondria of cells
- Used for low-intensity, long-duration activities

Marking Criteria:
3 marks: All key points covered with accurate terminology
2 marks: Most key points covered, minor inaccuracies
1 mark: Basic understanding shown
0 marks: Incorrect or no relevant content

Question 2: Explain how the aerobic system is used during a marathon (4 marks)
Assessment Objective: AO2 - Application
Topic: Energy Systems
Keywords: marathon, endurance, oxygen, sustained, long-duration
Acceptable Answer:
- Marathon is a long-duration activity (2+ hours)
- Aerobic system provides sustained energy throughout
- Runner breathes in oxygen continuously
- Oxygen used to break down glucose and fats for ATP
- Can maintain steady pace without lactic acid buildup

Marking Criteria:
4 marks: Clear explanation with application to marathon context
3 marks: Good explanation with some application
2 marks: Basic explanation, limited application
1 mark: Some relevant points
0 marks: Incorrect or irrelevant

Question 3: Evaluate the effectiveness of the aerobic system for different types of athletes (6 marks)
Assessment Objective: AO3 - Evaluation
Topic: Energy Systems
Keywords: evaluate, compare, advantages, disadvantages, effectiveness, athletes
Acceptable Answer:
- Highly effective for endurance athletes (marathon runners, cyclists, swimmers)
- Provides sustained energy without fatigue or lactic acid
- Less effective for power athletes (sprinters, weightlifters)
- Too slow for activities requiring quick energy bursts
- Advantages: Long-lasting energy, no toxic byproducts, efficient
- Disadvantages: Slow energy production, requires oxygen supply
- Comparison: Better for aerobic sports, worse for anaerobic sports

Marking Criteria:
5-6 marks: Comprehensive evaluation with multiple athlete types, clear advantages/disadvantages
3-4 marks: Good evaluation with some comparison
1-2 marks: Limited evaluation, basic points
0 marks: No evaluation or incorrect information

Question 4: Define SMART targets and give an example (4 marks)
Assessment Objective: AO1 - Knowledge
Topic: Goal Setting and SMART Targets
Keywords: SMART, specific, measurable, achievable, realistic, time-bound
Acceptable Answer:
- S = Specific (clear and precise goal)
- M = Measurable (can track progress with numbers/data)
- A = Achievable (realistic and attainable)
- R = Realistic (relevant to athlete's ability)
- T = Time-bound (has a deadline)
Example: "I will improve my 100m sprint time from 15 seconds to 13.5 seconds within 12 weeks"

Marking Criteria:
4 marks: All elements defined correctly with good example
3 marks: Most elements correct with example
2 marks: Some elements correct
1 mark: Basic understanding
0 marks: Incorrect

Question 5: Explain the difference between aerobic and anaerobic respiration (3 marks)
Assessment Objective: AO2 - Application
Topic: Energy Systems
Keywords: aerobic, anaerobic, oxygen, lactic acid, duration, intensity
Acceptable Answer:
- Aerobic uses oxygen, anaerobic doesn't
- Aerobic produces more ATP (38 vs 2)
- Aerobic = low intensity/long duration, Anaerobic = high intensity/short duration
- Aerobic has no toxic byproducts, Anaerobic produces lactic acid
- Aerobic takes longer to activate

Marking Criteria:
3 marks: Clear differences explained with accurate detail
2 marks: Main differences identified
1 mark: Basic difference mentioned
0 marks: Incorrect or no answer

Examiner Comments:
- Students often confuse aerobic and anaerobic systems
- Common mistake: stating aerobic system produces lactic acid
- Many students struggle to apply SMART targets with specific examples
- Evaluation questions require comparing multiple scenarios
- Look for use of technical terminology (ATP, mitochondria, lactic acid)
